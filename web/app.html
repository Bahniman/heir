The bet

The committee changes every year. Its debts should not vanish with it.

Running our own club's flagship event last year, we inherited club logins with almost no documentation, relearned speaker outreach from scratch, and left a promise to a speaker that nobody will carry into next year. Every club that hands over has its own version of this. Heir is our answer to who carries it.

The gap

Files get handed over. What was promised doesn't.

A GMP committee lasts one year, so every new team starts from a Drive folder, a chat history and a handover doc written by people on their way out. Our own club's speaker planning sheet from 2026 shows where the record breaks: it lists 319 possible speakers but records a status for only 26, and each of those says only "message sent". The replies stayed in members' own LinkedIn and WhatsApp threads.

Offices measure the same loss. In Panopto's 2018 survey of 1,000 US knowledge workers, people lost 5.3 hours a week waiting for colleagues' knowledge or recreating it, and 42% of what a person knew about their role was known only to them [1]. In a club, everyone who holds that knowledge leaves in the same month.

Universities see it too: student-life offices publish officer-transition guides because missing documentation and weak handoffs keep recurring [5]. The brief's Committee Grind track names the same pain.

What already exists

Commitment trackers exist. They follow a person, not a club.

We looked for tools that already do this. Each covers part of it. None keeps a club's obligations once the people who made them leave.

  • ToolWhat it doesWhy the debt still gets lost
  • Officer-transition guidesUniversity student-life offices [5]A checklist, a binder and a meeting between old and new officers.Written once by the people leaving. Nothing checks it against the mail.
  • Campus platformsAnthology Engage, CampusGroups [6]Move the officer roster and admin rights to the new team.They record who holds a role, not what the club owes.
  • Personal commitment trackersTown, WithOne's followup-debt [3][4]Read your own mail for promises you made and draft the follow-up.Scoped to one person. When that person graduates, the record leaves with them.
  • Email assistantsMicrosoft Viva daily briefing [2]Pull commitments and requests out of a person's email as reminders.Same personal scope, and no plan for the next event.
  • ChatGPT, Claude ProjectsAnswer questions about files someone uploads.They wait to be asked and never read the club's mail on a schedule.
  • HeirReads the club's shared mail, Drive and chat exports on a schedule into one ledger the club owns.Checks the handover doc against that ledger, plans the next event from it, and acts only after a member approves.

followup-debt uses the design closest to ours: a model finds the commitments and code validates them. Heir applies that idea to a club whose whole team changes every year.

How it works

Read, plan, then ask.

  1. 1 Before the handover

    Read everything once

    Three weeks before the handover, Heir reads last year's mail, Drive, chat export and forwarded notes into one ledger, and asks the outgoing team about every gap while they can still answer.

    Emailed Aditi: was the Quillstone report ever sent?

  2. 2 After the handover

    Plan from the record

    It plans the next event backwards from its date using lead times found in last year's mail, and holds back work that depends on an unpaid debt.

    Hall request due 3 Jul: 30 days' notice, plus a 5-day buffer

  3. 3 Every run

    Ask before acting

    Every email waits for a member's approval. Conflicting records, missing owners and silence after a follow-up go to the committee head.

    "Who owns last year's unsent report?"
SupervisorSets each run's goal and sends the others in
ScoutReads mail, Drive and chats into the ledger

PlannerDates every task and replans when one stalls
DrafterWrites every email for a member to approve

Where this is weakest

What a judge would ask.

  • Members won't forward their calls and DMs.

    Some won't. So capture is a single forward to the club bot, and Heir nudges the one owner of a stale contact instead of the whole group.

  • Messy email threads will trip the reader.

    They will. So a language model reads what the rules miss, code rejects any entry whose quote is not in the source, and every model entry stays marked "to reconfirm" until a member confirms it. Try it in the reader above.

  • It hasn't seen a real club mailbox.

    True. The next step is read-only access to one real club's mailbox for one shadow phase before its next handover.
