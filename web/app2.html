How Heir works

It's a web app you add to your home screen. No app store involved.

Each committee member gets an invite from the club's own email. They open the link, sign in with their campus Google account, and tap Add to Home Screen. From then on, Heir opens full screen like any other app and can send notifications. Anyone who skips that step still gets the same list as a morning email.

1

Push notification

Only for things that need you today. One tap opens the list.

iPhone needs iOS 16.4 or later and the app added to the Home Screen first. Android works from Chrome.1
2

7:00 email digest

Sent from the club mailbox to whoever has something to do. Its buttons open the same confirm screen.

Some mail scanners open links on their own, so a click in an email never sends anything by itself.
3

Escalation

When something stalls, like no reply or a missed date, Heir drafts a follow-up, then asks the committee head. The faculty advisor is the last stop.

Reply clock: 4 days. Escalation to the head is built; nudging members who ignore their own items, and the advisor step, are planned.

What one tap on "Approve and send" does

  1. You tapsigned in as you, on your phone
  2. Heir re-checksstill pending? anyone replied since? are you allowed?
  3. Undo window10 minutes in the real product, 4 seconds in this demo
  4. Sentfrom the club's Gmail, in the same thread
  5. Ledger updatedtask waits for reply, reply clock starts, debt marked paid
  6. Loggedwho approved what and when, visible to the committee

Other taps

  • Edit change the words, then approve. Heir sends your version.
  • Hold nothing is sent; the draft stays, marked held, with your name.
  • Answer a question your answer is saved as a confirmed fact and used in every run after.
  • Something's not right? pull the draft and mark the record wrong, or close it as handled.
  • Forward something to Heir paste or forward any message. A language model reads it against the ledger straight away; try it in the app.

Where it runs

  • Pilot Google Apps Script inside the club's own Google account: a 7:00 daily trigger, the ledger in a Google Sheet, mail through the club's Gmail. Members use the email digest and a web page.
  • Why that fits a club a free account allows 100 email recipients a day and 90 minutes of trigger time a day, 6 minutes per run.2 A club needs a fraction of that.
  • Later a hosted version with the installable app and push. Reading Gmail from a hosted server is a restricted Google scope and needs a security assessment; unverified apps are capped at 100 test users whose access expires every 7 days.3,4 That is why the pilot lives inside the club's own account.
  • Why not WhatsApp it needs a verified business number, and Meta charges per template message sent outside a 24-hour reply window.5 Possible later as an extra channel.

The phone screens in the game are the planned interface. The agents, ledger, checks and approvals behind them are the working prototype.

Heir reads in two ways: rules for the routine stuff, and a language model for anything new.

Every morning a rules engine reads the club's mail and files: deliverables, deadlines, lead times, promises, replies, silence. It's fast, free and gives the same answer every time, which is what you want for money and dates. But clubs also run on things no rule has seen: a voice note, a chat at midnight, a sponsor changing terms. Those go to a Claude model, and in this demo you can try it yourself: tap Forward something to Heir in the app.

1

The model gets tools, not power

It can search the ledger, read the plan, add a record, note a change, ask the head, and draft a reply. It cannot send, close a record, or change a status.

2

Code checks every write

Each action must quote the message word for word. A quote that is not there is rejected on screen. Drafts can only go to addresses already on record.

3

A person decides

What the model finds lands in Today as a question or a draft, marked "from your forward". Nothing leaves the club until someone taps approve.

What the live run needs

  • On claude.ai, signed in the page asks once for permission, then the model runs on your own Claude account, on Claude's fastest tier, so a forward takes seconds.
  • Anywhere else the four example messages replay steps Claude wrote ahead of time for them, clearly labelled, and the page still runs every gate. Your own text needs the live model.
  • In the product the same calls run inside the club's own setup, on the forward address and on mail the rules cannot read.

Heir will sometimes get things wrong, so it's built to fail safely.

Everything rests on three rules: nothing leaves the club without a person tapping approve, every line in the ledger keeps its source, and when two records disagree Heir asks instead of choosing.

The model proposes and code decides, so Heir gets more freedom only as fast as the club trusts it.

No one prompts Heir. It is built to wake on a schedule, its goal for each run follows the calendar (before the handover it collects what the leaving team knows, after it delivers the next event), and it hands every outside email and every real decision back to a person. Today a rules engine does the daily reading, so every run is repeatable and the club never depends on a model being up; the model reads what members forward. This is the order we would extend it in, each step starting from something that already runs.

Every year, student clubs lose track of what they owe.

A committee runs a club for a year, then hands it to strangers in a few weeks. What transfers is a doc, a Drive folder and some passwords. What doesn't: which sponsor is still waiting for a report, which speaker was promised a slot, how many days the venue office needs. Our own club's 2026 speaker sheet had 319 names, status for 26, no owner for 108, and 21 duplicates. Nobody did anything wrong. The knowledge lived in people's heads, and those people graduated.

Mission

Keep every promise a student club makes, after the people who made it have left.

Vision

A club's memory belongs to the club. Every handover passes on what is owed, not just the passwords.

Who it is for

  • The new head sees everything inherited on day one and makes the calls only a person should make.
  • Members see only their own items. One tap each.
  • The faculty advisor the last stop for escalations, and the one person who stays year to year.
  • Sponsors, speakers, campus offices deal with a club that remembers what it said.

What we hold ourselves to

  • A person sends. Heir drafts; nothing leaves without approval.
  • Every line has a source. If Heir cannot point to it, it does not claim it.
  • Unsure means ask. Conflicts go to a person, not to a guess.
  • The club owns the data. It lives in the club's account and outlasts every committee.

Roadmap

  1. NowWorking prototype on a mock club: five agents, a ledger that persists between runs, approvals, edge cases, tests, and a live model for messages no rule has seen.
  2. Nov 2026 to Mar 2027Pilot with our own club on its real mailbox, during the weeks before the April handover. Email digest and web page, on Apps Script.
  3. Apr to Aug 2027The first real handover with Heir, through one full event cycle. We count inherited items closed on time, items missed, and how often members flag Heir as wrong.
  4. After thatOther clubs on campus through the student council, the installable app with push, then other campuses.
